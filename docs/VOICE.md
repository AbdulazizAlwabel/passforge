# How Nicely Done talks

Nicely Done is made by one person — Abdulaziz — who got fed up with tool websites that let you work on
your file and then ask for money, or won’t let you download it. Every word on the site should sound
like him: a friendly, honest person explaining something to a neighbour.

## The rules

1. **Say what it does for you, not how it works.**
   “Join several PDFs into one” — not “Merge PDF documents client-side”.
2. **Short and plain.** One idea per sentence. Everyday words a 14-year-old or a grandparent understands.
3. **Talk to one person.** Use “you” and “your”. When Abdulaziz speaks (home note, About, What’s new), use “I”.
4. **Be honest about limits.** If something can’t be done, say so plainly. Never promise more than the tool does.
5. **No hype, no fear.** No “revolutionary”, “ultimate”, “military-grade”. No scary warnings unless there is a real risk.
6. **Privacy in human words.** “Your files never leave your phone or computer” — not “processed locally in the browser”.
7. **Technical details are welcome, but second.** Put encryption names, DPI, algorithms under a small
   “For the technical folks” note or in the FAQ — never in the headline.
8. **Buttons say what happens.** “Download PDF”, “Check this link”, “Make a password” — not “Submit”, “Process”, “Go”.

## Words

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| client-side, in-browser, locally | on your device · on your phone or computer |
| zero-knowledge | only you can open it — not even me |
| encrypt | lock · scramble (mention “encrypted” once, in the details) |
| upload / process | add · open · fix |
| utility, toolkit | tools |
| leverage, seamless, robust | (just don’t) |

## Arabic

Warm, simple Modern Standard Arabic that anyone in Saudi Arabia reads comfortably — the way a kind
person would explain it, not the language of a contract. Short sentences, common words
(«ملفاتك لا تغادر جهازك» rather than «تتم المعالجة محليًا»). Keep brand and product names in Latin
letters (Nicely Done, PDF). Abdulaziz signs as «عبدالعزيز».
