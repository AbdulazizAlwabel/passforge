Private by design

  • You test against our own server in Riyadh — no Ookla, Cloudflare or other third party ever sees your connection.
  • Nothing is logged, and results never leave your browser.
  • The test data is random bytes, generated fresh and thrown away.

How it measures

  • Latency and jitter from a series of tiny requests.
  • Download and upload with four parallel streams, ignoring the first second while the connection ramps up.
  • “Under load” repeats the latency check while the line is busy — the lag you feel in calls and games.

Why a self-hosted speed test?

Popular speed tests are run by companies that collect your IP address, location and results to build network maps and ad profiles. This one runs entirely between your browser and our server, and the result exists only on your screen.

Because it measures the path to a single server in Riyadh, it’s most representative for connections in Saudi Arabia and the Gulf. Very fast fibre plans (above roughly 600 Mbps) may read a little lower than their real capacity.

Why is my result lower than my plan?

Wi-Fi is the most common bottleneck — try again on a cable or close to the router. Other devices downloading at the same time, VPNs and busy evening hours also reduce speed.

What is “responsiveness under load”?

It’s how much your latency rises while the connection is busy, often called bufferbloat. A good router keeps it low, so video calls and games stay smooth even while someone is downloading.
