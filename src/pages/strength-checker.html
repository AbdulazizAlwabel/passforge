Private, even the leak check

Your password never leaves this page. To check if it has leaked, only 5 scrambled characters are sent to Have I Been Pwned — never the password itself. Nobody, including me, learns what you typed.

What makes a password weak?

People who break into accounts don’t guess randomly. They start with lists of passwords leaked from other websites, then try common words with small tweaks (p@ssw0rd), keyboard patterns (qwerty), years and dates. A password can look complicated and still fall in seconds if it follows one of these habits.

The fix is simple: make it long and random. Let the Password Generator make one for you, and keep them in the vault.

Why does a leaked password matter?

If your password showed up in a data breach, attackers try it first — no matter how strong it looks. The check compares it against more than 900 million leaked passwords, without ever sending it.

For the technical folks

Entropy starts from length × character pool and is reduced for dictionary words, sequences, repeats, keyboard walks and dates. Crack times assume the attacker finds it halfway through on average. The leak check uses k-anonymity: the first 5 characters of the SHA-1 hash go to Have I Been Pwned and the matching is done here.
