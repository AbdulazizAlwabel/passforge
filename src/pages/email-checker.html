How do I get the full source? (it makes the check much stronger)
  • Gmail (computer): open the email → ⋮ → Show original → Copy to clipboard.
  • Outlook: open the email → ⋯ → View → View message source.
  • Apple Mail (Mac): View → Message → Raw Source.
  • Yahoo Mail: ⋯ → View raw message.
  • iPhone and Android mail apps can’t show the source. Paste the text you see, and type the sender’s address in the box above.

How it stays private

  • The email is read right here in your browser. It isn’t uploaded, saved or logged.
  • To check the links against threat lists, only the first 5 characters of a scrambled fingerprint of each link are sent — the same way as the Link Checker.
  • Nothing in the email is opened or loaded: no images, no links, no attachments.

What it looks for

  • Who really sent it: fake bank and government names, look-alike addresses, free email accounts pretending to be a company.
  • Links that show one address but go to another, and links on known threat lists.
  • Pressure tricks in Arabic and English: threats to close your account, requests for passwords or codes, prizes and fees.
  • Dangerous attachments and disguised file names.

Is this email really from my bank?

Scam emails copy the look of a bank, Absher, Saudi Post or Amazon almost perfectly — the logo, the colours, even the signature. What they can’t copy is where they really come from and where their links really go. This checker looks at exactly those things, plus the pressure tricks scammers rely on.

When you paste the full source, it can also read the checks your own email provider already did — called SPF, DKIM and DMARC. If an email says it’s from your bank and passes DMARC for the bank’s real domain, it really came from them. If it fails, someone is pretending.

No checker can promise an email is safe. If something asks for a password, a code or money, contact the company yourself — by typing their address or calling the number on your card — not through the email.

For the technical folks

The message is parsed in the browser: headers are unfolded and RFC 2047 encoded words decoded; MIME parts are walked recursively with base64 and quoted-printable decoding and the declared charset. HTML parts go through DOMParser, which builds an inert document — nothing is fetched or run. The topmost Authentication-Results header is read for SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Links use the same rules and k-anonymous threat lookup as the Link Checker.
